"I wanted my kids to grow up with the stories I never had. So I started writing them."

I'm David Bergqvist, a Swedish dad and builder. Today I live with my family in the Languedoc in the south of France, where I build wooden window shutters. Before that I built my own house on Vrångö, an island off the Swedish west coast, and ran my own businesses. Along the way I learned things about work, money and serving others that no one taught me as a child.

It's no accident that Penny's dad is a carpenter. It's my trade too.

When I looked for books to teach my own kids those things, I found two kinds: shiny entertainment with nothing underneath, or well-meaning lessons kids tune out after one page. I wanted both. A story that grips them, and truth that stays with them.

So I write the books I wish I'd grown up with. Every one of them is something I'd read to my own kids tonight.

David

How I make the books

Yes, I use AI. Here's how.

I want to be open about this, because you deserve to know what you're reading with your child.

I use AI as a tool: for research, to develop material, and as a creative partner when I try out ideas. I work closely with my own AI agent, which lets me make really good books, efficiently.

For me, a book isn't about which tool you use. It's about the result you want: a story that holds a child's attention, a lesson that's true, and a faith that shows in how people live.

That's why nothing goes out the way the first draft looked. I read every book very carefully, check facts, Bible verses and names, and keep editing until I'm proud of the result.

Then the books meet my small test panel (my kids), aged 3, 5 and 9. So far the ratings have been good.

I hope you and your child will love the books.

Questions parents ask

Good to know

Are the books preachy?

No. The story always comes first. Faith shows up in how characters think, pray and choose, with a short Bible verse at the key moments. Kids discover the lessons themselves instead of being told.

Isn't money a strange topic for kids?

Kids learn about money anyway, mostly from ads and screens. Our books teach that money is a receipt for helping someone, never the most important thing, and that generosity and faithfulness matter more than getting rich.

Which book should we start with?

Ages 3 to 6: Penny and the Seed. Ages 7 to 10: Penny and the Golden Notebook, then Ben. Ages 11 to 14: Maya. Ages 15 to 18: The Condition. Every series starts with a book that stands on its own.

Do the books work for read-aloud?

Yes. Short chapters that end with a question or a surprise make them ideal for bedtime, and each book ends with discussion questions for the whole family.

Where can we buy them?

On Amazon, as paperback and Kindle. Join the free pack list and we'll tell you the day each new book comes out.

Do you use AI to make the books?

Yes, as a tool for research, developing material and trying out ideas. I read every book very carefully and edit it until I'm proud of the result, and the books are tested by my small test panel, aged 3, 5 and 9. Read how I work.
