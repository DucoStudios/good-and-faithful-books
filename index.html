"I wanted my kids to grow up with the stories I never had. So we started writing them."

We're David and Emelie, a Swedish family of builders and makers, now living in the south of France. David has built a house with his own hands and run his own businesses, and Emelie runs her own jewelry brand, PS46 Jewelry. Along the way we learned things about work, money and serving others that no one taught us as children.

When we looked for books to teach our own kids those things, we found two kinds: shiny entertainment with nothing underneath, or well-meaning lessons kids tune out after one page. We wanted both. A story that grips them, and truth that stays with them.

So we wrote the books we wish we'd grown up with. Every one of them is something we'd read to our own kids tonight.

David & Emelie

Questions parents ask

Good to know

Are the books preachy?

No. The story always comes first. Faith shows up in how characters think, pray and choose, with a short Bible verse at the key moments. Kids discover the lessons themselves instead of being told.

Isn't money a strange topic for kids?

Kids learn about money anyway, mostly from ads and screens. Our books teach that money is a receipt for helping someone, never the most important thing, and that generosity and faithfulness matter more than getting rich.

Which book should we start with?

Ages 3 to 6: Penny and the Seed. Ages 7 to 10: Penny and the Golden Notebook, then Ben. Ages 11 to 14: Maya. Ages 15 to 18: The Condition. Every series starts with a book that stands on its own.

Do the books work for read-aloud?

Yes. Short chapters that end with a question or a surprise make them ideal for bedtime, and each book ends with discussion questions for the whole family.

Where can we buy them?

On Amazon, as paperback and Kindle, and free to read with Kindle Unlimited. Join the free pack list and we'll tell you the day each new book comes out.
