The story

Thirty days. Twenty dollars. Create as much value as you can.

Maya Fowler, 13, finds an old key in her bike basket with a card: If you see a problem no one else sees, come to the Water Tower on Thursday at 7 p.m. Alone.

Inside the old water tower in Bayview sits the Council: five older people who have each built something from nothing. Every four years they choose four young people and give them the Tower Challenge. Thirty days, twenty dollars each. Meanwhile, Maya's mom's little café, Linda's, is going under since a chain opened across the street.

What your teen takes away

People pay for what it does for them

Maya learns to stop selling what she makes and start solving what people need.

The bigger the problem, the bigger the value

The Council keeps pushing her to look past easy wins to the problems that really matter.

What you do when no one is watching

Winning starts to look different when integrity is on the line, and her rival will do almost anything to win.

Questions parents ask

What age is Maya and the Tower Challenge for?

Readers 11 to 14. It's a full middle grade novel with a fast plot, a rival, a secret council and a family café in trouble.

When does it come out?

In 2027. Join the family list and we'll tell you the day it's out.

Is it a Christian book?

Yes. Faith shows up in Maya's grandmother, who prays for her every night, in Maya's own prayers when she doesn't know what to do, and in a verse from Proverbs carved over the tower door: A good name is more desirable than great riches. The story never preaches.

Why does the list send a pack for younger kids?

Our list is where we announce every new book. The free pack is made for kids 6 to 10, so it's a gift for younger siblings, and you'll hear first when Maya comes out.
