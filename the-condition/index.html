The story

Take the money, or take the test

Alex Mercer, 17, inherits from his great-great-uncle Gus, a bachelor who lived simply in a converted boathouse by the harbor and always wore the same blue work jacket. Everyone thought he owned nothing.

The will gives Alex a choice. Option A: twenty-five thousand dollars today. Option B: a year as an apprentice to the executor, Edith Langley, and five conditions, one at a time. Meet them all and he gets "the rest". Fail one and he gets nothing. His family needs the money now.

The five conditions

  1. The hands. Work a month at the simplest job there is, for the lowest wage. Save every cent.
  2. The head. In eight weeks, learn something someone will pay more for.
  3. The team. Lead others. Get them to do something better than you could alone.
  4. The ownership. Own something that runs for a week without you.
  5. The seed. Let money work for you, and teach someone else to do the same.

None of it is theory. Every condition is a real task with a deadline, and the kind of lesson most of us wish someone had given us at seventeen.

Questions parents ask

What age is The Condition for?

Teens 15 to 18, and parents who want something to talk about with them. It reads like a novel, not a finance course.

When does it come out?

In 2028. Join the family list and we'll tell you the day it's out.

Is it a Christian book?

Yes. Gus quotes Proverbs in the margins of his letters, and the real question of the book is what money is for. The story never preaches.
