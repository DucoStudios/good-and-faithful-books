The story

Five clues, one old mill, and a boy who wants to do everything himself

Ben Walker, 10, builds things out of anything he can find. His great-grandfather Edwin was the town inventor, with a workshop in the old mill by the river. Now Millbrook wants to tear the mill down after Midsummer and turn it into a parking lot, unless someone can show that it's needed.

Then Ben finds Edwin's map: a yellowed sheet with the mill in the middle and five small drawings around it. Each one leads to something Edwin built that is still in use, and to a sentence carved in wood or metal. It all starts when Sadie, 82, can't open a jar on her own, and Ben spends two hours making one jar opener.

What your child takes away

Hands reach one

Ben can make one jar opener at a time. It's good work, but it will never be enough.

Tools and knowledge reach many

When he learns why things break and builds a jig, ten minutes does the work of two hours.

Together you lift more

Ben stops doing everything himself and teaches his classmates to build. What he plants keeps growing without him.

Questions parents ask

What age is Ben and the Inventor's Map for?

Kids 7 to 10, especially kids who love to build, take things apart and invent. It also works as a family read-aloud.

When does it come out?

In 2027, after the first Penny books. Join the free pack list and we'll tell you the day it's out.

Is it a Christian book?

Yes. Faith lives in Edwin's notebook, in Ella's prayers and in the town church, which is one of the stops on the map. The story never preaches.

Is the free pack for Ben readers too?

Yes. The pack is made for kids 6 to 10, with puzzles, a maze and conversation cards about work, giving and saving.
